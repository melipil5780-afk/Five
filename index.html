My Story

From adrift
to present.

I didn’t start out knowing I’d become a therapist. As a young adult, I found myself stranded in a Midwestern college town after a bad breakup — no plan, no direction, no real reason to stay. A Bachelor’s degree in English, some experience in food service, a 1971 Chevy Nova, and a restlessness I couldn’t name.

Everything in me said to leave. To move. To find something better somewhere else. But something quieter said: stay. Not because things were good. They weren’t. But because the answers I was looking for weren’t going to be found in the next city.

I stayed. I sat with myself. And when I was ready, I found a therapist — and something shifted. The act of “staying in one place” became “staying with” a person. And “staying with” a person became “staying with” a process. That distinction — between running from and being with — has shaped everything I do.

I have spent decades since then learning what it means to truly be present with another person. To stay with them in the difficult, the confusing, the stuck. To trust that something real can happen when two people sit together and don’t rush toward a solution.

My approach is gestalt and process-oriented, which means we work with what is alive in the room — not just what you report from the outside. I am also a clinical supervisor, which means I have spent years helping other therapists find their footing. I bring that same thoughtfulness to my work with clients.

Outside the office, I am a father, a partner, an amateur classical guitarist, and an avid reader of poetry. I believe a good life is built from the same things a good session is: attention, honesty, and the willingness to stay.

How I Work

Not a method.
A way of being together.

01
We work in the present

Gestalt therapy is not about excavating the past. It’s about noticing what is happening right now — in your body, in this room, between us — and following that thread. The past lives in the present. That’s where we meet it.

02
The relationship is the work

I don’t believe therapy is something I do to you. I believe it happens between us. The quality of our contact — the honesty, the moments of friction, the repair — is not incidental to healing. It is the healing.

03
Slowness as a practice

Most of us move through life at a pace that keeps us from actually feeling it. I work slowly and deliberately. I am not in a hurry to fix or reframe. I am more interested in what happens when we stop rushing — and stay.

The person who feels stuck.

Maybe you know something needs to change, but you don’t know what. Maybe you’ve been managing fine for years — and fine is no longer enough. Maybe you keep arriving at the same place no matter how hard you try to move forward. That stuckness is not a character flaw. It is often a signal worth listening to. That’s the kind of work I find most meaningful.

Those navigating recovery.

I have a particular depth of experience with people in long-term addiction recovery — not the early crisis phase, but the years after, when the substance is gone and the deeper questions surface. Who am I now? What do I actually feel? What do I want my life to look like? Those questions deserve real attention, and I am well-equipped to hold them with you.

People new to therapy.

First experiences in therapy matter enormously. If you’ve never done this before, I want you to know: you don’t need to arrive with the right words or a clear problem to solve. You just need to show up. I will meet you exactly where you are.

Honesty First

Who I may not be right for.

I think it is worth being honest about this. I work best with people who are willing to slow down and stay with discomfort rather than move quickly past it. If you are looking for structured homework, skills-based worksheets, or a highly directive approach, I am probably not your best fit — and I would rather tell you that now than waste your time.

I also work exclusively with adults, and I do not provide crisis or emergency services. If you are in acute crisis, please reach out to a crisis line or emergency service before contacting me.

That said — if any part of what you’ve read here resonates, I would genuinely encourage you to reach out. The only way to know if we are a good fit is to talk.

What to Expect

Your first
session.

The first session is not a test. There is nothing you need to perform or prove. Most people feel some combination of nervous and relieved. Both are completely fine.

We will use the time to get oriented — to each other, and to what brings you here. You don’t need a prepared speech. Just come as you are.

1
We talk about what brought you here

Not a full history. Just what’s present. What is making you reach out now, in this moment of your life? That’s usually enough to begin.

2
I share how I work

I’ll describe my approach honestly, including what I am good at and what I am not. I want you to be able to make an informed decision about whether to continue.

3
You ask me whatever you want

Seriously. About my background, my style, my fees, my experience with something specific. There are no questions that are off-limits in a first session.

4
We decide together how to move forward

No pressure. No commitment from this conversation. If it feels right to both of us, we schedule a second session. If not, I will do my best to point you toward someone who is a better fit.
